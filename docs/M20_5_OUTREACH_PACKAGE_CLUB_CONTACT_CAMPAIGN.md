# M20.5 — Outreach Package & Club Contact Campaign

## Purpose
Create a repeatable, permission-first outreach process for WFM club data relationships. Outreach must never imply that a club has granted rights unless the tracker records documented permission.

## Outreach package

### 1. First-contact email

**Subject:** Women’s Football Market — Data Partnership & Club Permission Request

Hello [Name/Team],

I’m reaching out on behalf of Women’s Football Market (WFM), an independent women’s football data platform focused on helping clubs, scouts, agents, researchers and other football professionals discover and investigate player and club information.

We are building WFM with an evidence-first approach and would like to establish a direct relationship with [Club] so that information associated with the club can be represented accurately and, where applicable, used with the club’s permission.

We would like to discuss permission for the following categories, with each category treated separately:

- Club and team information
- Player roster information
- Player performance/statistical information
- Transfer information
- Contract information
- Salary information
- Club logos/marks
- Player or club photographs
- Commercial use of permitted data

We are not asking the club to confirm information it does not wish to disclose. Where information is not authorized, WFM can leave it unpublished, restrict its use, or identify it as coming from another permitted source.

If appropriate, we would be happy to provide a short overview of WFM, the proposed data scope, attribution language, and the permission categories for your review.

Could you please direct me to the person responsible for data, communications, legal/licensing, media, or commercial partnerships?

Thank you,

Oscar Rojas
Women’s Football Market (WFM)

### 2. Short explanation

WFM is designed as a structured research and scouting platform rather than a replacement for a club’s official website. The goal is to organize discoverable football information, preserve source provenance, distinguish verified from reported information, and give clubs a clear way to define what WFM may publish or use commercially.

### 3. Permission categories

Each category should be approved independently:

| Category | Requested scope |
|---|---|
| Roster | Current/historical player association with the club |
| Stats | Match/season performance data |
| Contracts | Contract dates/status where the club authorizes publication |
| Salaries | Compensation figures only where specifically authorized |
| Transfers | Transfer/registration movement information |
| Photos | Specific images and stated usage rights |
| Logos | Club marks and stated usage rights |
| Commercial use | Only the categories explicitly permitted |
| Attribution | Required wording/link if specified by the club |

Facts, database rights, trademarks, photographs, contractual terms, and licensing rights should not be treated as one permission category. WFM should record the exact scope and restrictions communicated by the rights holder.

## Attribution

Default language until a club specifies otherwise:

Data and club information provided or authorized by [Club], where applicable. WFM also identifies independent source provenance for information obtained from other sources.

Do not use this language as evidence of permission. The tracker remains the authoritative record of permission status.

## Campaign workflow

1. Research the appropriate club contact.
2. Record contact details and source URL in the club permission tracker.
3. Set outreach status to researching_contact or drafted.
4. Send the first-contact message.
5. Log the contact event and date.
6. Set a follow-up date.
7. If the club responds, record the response and requested scope.
8. Attach or reference supporting documentation where available.
9. Translate the documented response into individual permission fields.
10. Keep unsupported categories unknown, restricted, or declined; never infer approval.
11. If commercial rights are granted, create/link the corresponding licensing record.
12. Review the tracker before publishing affected data.

## Follow-up cadence

Suggested operational cadence:

- Initial outreach: Day 0
- First follow-up: Day 7
- Second follow-up: Day 21
- Final follow-up: Day 45
- After final follow-up: no_response unless the club re-engages

This is an operational suggestion, not a statement of club policy.

## Pilot campaign

Start with clubs where WFM already has meaningful data coverage. This allows the first conversations to demonstrate an existing, reviewable dataset rather than requesting permission around a hypothetical product.

Recommended pilot criteria:
- Active club in WFM
- Existing player/roster coverage
- Existing competition relationship
- Existing source provenance
- Clear official contact channel
- Potential value from accurate attribution and direct club relationship

Do not mark a club as permission_granted merely because it is a pilot club.

## Documentation standard

For every affirmative permission decision, preserve:

- Who granted or communicated the permission
- Their role/organization
- Date
- Communication channel
- Exact categories approved
- Commercial-use scope
- Attribution requirements
- Restrictions
- Start/end dates if applicable
- Agreement/reference document
- Any renewal or expiration condition

## Important distinction

WFM’s internal source record and club permission record serve different purposes:

- Source record: where a particular piece of information came from.
- Permission record: what WFM is authorized to publish/use and under what conditions.

A source being official does not automatically mean every possible reuse right has been granted. Conversely, a permission agreement does not eliminate the need to preserve source provenance.
